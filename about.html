Artist Biography

Loh Ek Sem

Born 1956, Malaysia. Fine artist and cultural documentarian preserving vanishing kampung life and Peranakan heritage through four decades of painting and paper cast sculpture.

Life & Work

Loh Ek Sem (b.1956) is a Malaysian fine artist whose four-decade practice has been driven by a singular mission: to document the vanishing cultural landscapes of Southeast Asia. His work functions as visual anthropology—art that preserves not just aesthetic objects, but entire ways of life disappearing from modern Malaysia.

Loh Ek Sem in his studio

Sem began his career in the advertising industry, where he developed technical mastery and a keen eye for visual storytelling. But in 1994, he made a decisive pivot to fine art, driven by a growing urgency to capture the kampung (village) life he remembered from his youth—traditional wooden houses on stilts, community rituals, the textures and rhythms of rural Malaysia that rapid development was replacing.

Over the next decade, Sem produced his flagship Malay Kampung Series—large-scale acrylic paintings that depict kampung scenes with documentary precision and nostalgic warmth. Works like By the Well (137 × 214cm) and Fishing Village (122 × 186cm) are not mere representations; they are time capsules of a world that no longer exists in physical form.

Loh Ek Sem — connection to rural life

Parallel to his kampung documentation, Sem developed a deep engagement with Peranakan heritage—the hybrid Chinese-Malay culture that flourished in colonial Malaya. His Peranakan Series captures the intricate details of Nyonya life: ceremonial dress, festival preparations, the domestic rituals that defined a community straddling multiple identities.

Loh Ek Sem — artistic inspiration travels

In the 2000s, Sem invented an entirely new medium: paper cast sculpture. Dissatisfied with painting's inability to capture the roughness of kampung materials—aged wood, attap roofing, hand-carved furniture—he developed a seven-step process that transforms recycled paper pulp into three-dimensional relief sculptures. The technique, which takes weeks to complete for each work, produces pieces that are neither painting nor sculpture, but both at once.

Loh Ek Sem at exhibition

Today, Sem continues to work from his studio in Johor Bahru, maintaining an archive of over 100 works across four series. His art has been exhibited in Singapore, Paris, and New York through Opera Gallery (1995–2000), and his monumental Cultural Pin Carnival mural (2009)—created with 3.7 million map pins—stands as a testament to his belief that art can be both culturally significant and publicly accessible.

The Paper Cast Process

Loh Ek Sem invented his signature paper cast technique in the 2000s, seeking a medium that could capture the roughness of kampung materials—aged wood, attap roofing, hand-carved furniture—that painting alone could not convey. The process takes weeks for each work, transforming recycled paper into durable sculptural reliefs.

1. Drawing

The image is first drawn on wood block or board, establishing the composition and outlines.

2. Mould Making

A mould is carved or formed to create the three-dimensional base for the paper cast.

3. Paper Preparation

Recycled paper is soaked, blended into pulp, and mixed with binding agents.

4. Carving & Shaping

Details are carved into the wood block or the paper pulp is shaped while wet.

5. Drying

The paper cast air-dries over weeks. Rushing causes cracking or warping.

6. Demoulding

Once fully cured, the paper cast is carefully removed from the mould.

7. Coloring

Acrylic paints are applied to bring the scene to life, building up layers of color.

8. Finishing

Final details and protective coatings complete the transformation from paper to art.

In His Own Words
"The kampung houses I painted in the 1990s no longer exist in reality— replaced by condominiums and highways. But they live on in my archive: attap roofs, wooden stilts, the smell of woodsmoke and coconut oil. These paintings are not just art; they are time capsules." — Loh Ek Sem
"How do you capture the texture of an aged wooden door, the roughness of attap roofing, the grain of hand-carved furniture? Paint wasn't enough. I developed paper casting over five years: recycled paper, hand-carved moulds, natural pigments. The result is neither painting nor sculpture— both at once." — Loh Ek Sem on paper cast technique
"A 20-year-old KL native might never have seen a kampung house in person. But in my gallery, they encounter the architecture, the community rituals, the textures of their ancestors' lives. The art becomes a bridge—connecting generations who share a culture separated by time." — Loh Ek Sem on cultural preservation
