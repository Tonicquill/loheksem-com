Painting Series

Peranakan

Nyonya heritage, ceremonial dress, festival preparations, and the domestic rituals of Peranakan culture—a hybrid Chinese-Malay identity that flourished in colonial Malaya and continues to influence Malaysian society.

The Peranakan Series explores the rich visual culture of the Peranakan (Straits Chinese) community—descendants of Chinese traders who settled in Malaya and married local Malay women. The resulting hybrid culture, known as Peranakan or Baba-Nyonya, developed distinctive traditions in dress, cuisine, language, and religious practice.

Sem's paintings document key moments in Peranakan life: the arrival of Admiral Cheng Ho (the legendary Chinese explorer who visited Malacca in the 15th century), festival preparations (Mooncake Festival, Chinese New Year), wedding ceremonies, and domestic scenes that reveal the intricate material culture of Nyonya households.

The series includes 10 major works, painted between 1996 and 2002. Like the Malay Kampung Series, these are executed in acrylic on canvas—chosen for its ability to capture both the fine detail of embroidered textiles and the bold colors of ceremonial objects.

Cultural Context

Who Are the Peranakan?

The term Peranakan comes from the Malay root anak (child), meaning "locally born." Peranakans are descendants of Chinese traders who settled in Malaya (primarily Malacca, Penang, and Singapore) and married local Malay women. The community developed a hybrid culture that blended Chinese ancestry with Malay language, dress, and customs.

Men were called Baba and women Nyonya (or Nonya). The Nyonya women developed distinctive clothing: the kebaya (embroidered blouse) and sarong (batik wrap skirt), along with elaborate beaded shoes called kasut manek.

Peranakan Traditions Depicted

  • Cheng Ho's legacy — Admiral Zheng He's seven voyages (1405–1433) established Chinese presence in Southeast Asia. His arrival in Malacca is commemorated in Peranakan folklore as the beginning of the community.
  • Mooncake Festival — The Mid-Autumn Festival (15th day of the 8th lunar month) celebrates the harvest. Peranakan celebrations include mooncakes, lantern processions, and the Nyonya specialty: kuih bakul (sticky rice cake).
  • Peranakan weddings — A 12-day ceremony incorporating Chinese and Malay rituals. Key elements include the tea ceremony (Chinese) and bersanding (Malay-style throne seating). The bride wears a kebaya with kasut manek (beaded shoes she embroidered herself).
  • Nyonya cuisine — A fusion of Chinese ingredients and Malay spices. Signature dishes include laksa (spicy noodle soup), ayam buah keluak (chicken with Indonesian nuts), and kuih (colorful bite-sized sweets).

Why This Series Matters

Peranakan culture is often romanticized as "exotic" or "quaint." But Sem's paintings treat it as a living tradition—one that shaped Malaysian identity in profound ways. The Peranakan community produced some of Malaya's most influential figures: merchants, politicians, artists, and writers who navigated multiple cultural worlds.

By documenting Peranakan rituals and material culture, Sem preserves not just aesthetic objects but a way of life that continues to influence contemporary Malaysia. The kebaya, the kuih, the festival celebrations— these are not relics. They are practices that continue to define what it means to be Malaysian.

Documentation Archive

Artist's Documentation

Each painting in the Peranakan Series is accompanied by detailed documentation written by the artist. These documents provide deeper insights into the creative process, cultural research, and personal reflections behind each work.

The archive contains 46 documentation files covering:

  • Wedding rituals — Chiu Thau, Tea Ceremony, Wedding Procession, The Bridal Chamber
  • Festival celebrations — Chinese New Year, Chap Goh Meh, Mooncake Festival, Dragon Boat Festival
  • Domestic life — Embroidery sessions, Home tutoring, Making soya drinks, Ironing clothes
  • Family relationships — Grandmother with grandchildren, Mother care, Family gatherings
  • Cultural practices — The Berandam (coming of age), Seterika (ironing), Special Treatment

Documentation files are available in the artist's archive at assets/Peranakan/docs/
