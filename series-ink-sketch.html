Drawings & Sketches

Ink & Sketch

Urban drawings documenting street life across Malaysia — from the bustling lanes of Petaling Street to the quiet corners of Malacca River, captured in ink and pencil.

The Ink & Sketch series (2000–2015) represents Loh Ek Sem's most immediate and spontaneous work. Unlike the carefully composed paintings that require weeks to complete, these drawings capture moments in real time — the energy of a crowded market, the rhythm of a busy street, the quiet dignity of everyday commerce.

Sem works primarily in ink on paper, a medium that demands confidence and speed. There is no room for hesitation — each line is permanent, each mark a commitment. The results are drawings that feel alive, vibrating with the energy of the scenes they depict.

The series includes 50+ works on paper, ranging from quick field sketches (28 × 38cm) to larger finished drawings. Subjects include Petaling Street market, Penang lanes, Malacca River scenes, hawker centres, and the shop house architecture of Malaysian urban life — all documented with the artist's characteristic attention to cultural and architectural detail.

Technique Notes

Ink Drawing as Documentation

Ink drawing demands speed and confidence. Unlike painting, there is no room for revision — each line is permanent. This makes ink ideal for capturing scenes that are themselves in motion: markets, streets, crowds.

  • Direct observation — Most drawings are done on site, often in challenging conditions. The artist must work quickly before the scene changes.
  • Economy of line — Every mark must count. There is no space for hesitation or second-guessing.
  • Ink wash — Diluted ink creates tonal variation, suggesting depth and atmosphere without detailed rendering.
  • Mixed media — Some works combine ink with watercolour or pencil, adding color accents to the monochrome foundation.

Why Drawing Matters

Photography captures a moment instantly. Painting captures a moment over weeks. Drawing occupies a middle ground — fast enough to capture movement, slow enough to require deep observation.

These drawings document Malaysian urban life at a specific moment in history. The shop houses, the street markets, the river scenes — all are changing rapidly. In fifty years, much of what is depicted here will be gone. The drawings preserve not just the visual appearance but the feeling of being there: the noise, the smells, the energy.

Sem's drawings are in the tradition of artist-documentarians — from Daumier's Paris to Hokusai's Edo. They are not idealized views but honest records of everyday life, drawn with the affection that comes from deep familiarity.
