Sculptural Relief

Paper Cast

An invented technique: recycled paper pulp, hand-carved moulds, natural pigments. The result is neither painting nor sculpture—both at once. A medium developed over five years to capture what paint alone could not.

The 7-Step Process

Paper Cast is a unique medium pioneered by Loh Ek Sem—transforming recycled paper pulp into three-dimensional relief sculptures that capture the texture and spirit of traditional crafts. Each work takes 2–3 weeks to complete.

Step 01

Pencil Drawing

Initial reference sketch on paper—establishing composition, proportions, and key details before any three-dimensional work begins.

Step 02

Wood Carving

Creating the mould base by carving wood blocks. The carved surface will become the negative space for the paper cast.

Step 03

Mould Making

Preparing the form with silicone or rubber layer over the carved wood. This flexible mould will release the finished cast without damage.

Step 04

Paper Laying

Recycled paper is soaked, mashed into pulp, and pressed into the mould by hand. The pulp captures every detail of the carved surface.

Step 05

Drying

Natural air drying over 5–7 days. Rushing this step causes warping or cracking. The slow drying allows even moisture evaporation.

Step 06

Detaching

Carefully separating the dried paper cast from the mould. The flexible mould allows release without tearing the fragile paper form.

Step 07

Colouring

Hand-painting with natural pigments. Multiple layers build up color depth. The porous paper surface absorbs pigment differently than canvas.
